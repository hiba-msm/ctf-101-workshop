# Writeups and Learning

[Home](../../README.md) › **Writeups and learning** &nbsp;·&nbsp; See also: [Further reading](FURTHER-READING.md) · [Practice platforms](../learning-path/PRACTICE-PLATFORMS.md)

A **writeup** is an explanation of how someone solved a challenge. Reading writeups is how most CTF players learn new techniques, and writing your own is how you make them stick.

---

## How to read a writeup (without just copying the answer)

1. **Try first.** Give the challenge an honest 20–40 minutes before searching.
2. **Read only until you're unstuck.** Stop at the first idea you hadn't thought of, close the writeup and try again.
3. **Ask "how would I have known?"** What clue led the author to that step? That's the reusable lesson.
4. **Re-solve it yourself** from scratch the next day, without the writeup.
5. **Write down the technique** (not the flag) in your notes, with the category and a one-line summary.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **During a live competition, don't look for writeups of that competition's challenges, and don't share flags or solutions** until it ends. Most CTF rules forbid it, and it spoils the event for others. Writeups are for after the competition, for archived challenges and for practice platforms.

## How to write your own

A good beginner writeup has:

1. **Challenge name, category, points, event.**
2. **What you were given** (files, description).
3. **What you tried**, including dead ends (they're valuable to readers).
4. **The step that worked and why**, with commands or screenshots.
5. **What you learned**, in one or two sentences.

Keep writeups in a Git repository or a blog. They become a portfolio employers like to see. Only publish writeups after the competition ends and if the platform allows it (some, like OverTheWire, ask players not to spoil levels).

## Learning by watching

Free YouTube channels popular with CTF learners. Watch actively: pause and try before the solution.

| Channel | Best for |
|---|---|
| [John Hammond](https://www.youtube.com/@_JohnHammond) | CTF walkthroughs across all categories, beginner-friendly |
| [LiveOverflow](https://www.youtube.com/@LiveOverflow/playlists) | Deep, clear explanations: binary exploitation, web, "how hackers think" |
| [PwnFunction](https://www.youtube.com/@PwnFunction) | Short animated explanations of web vulnerabilities |
| [IppSec](https://www.youtube.com/@ippsec) | Detailed walkthroughs of retired training machines; methodology |
| [NetworkChuck](https://www.youtube.com/@NetworkChuck) | Beginner networking and Linux, very approachable |

## Communities

- **Your university's cyber security club**: the best place to find a team.
- **Platform communities:** CyLab Security Academy, TryHackMe, CryptoHack, OverTheWire and Root-Me each run community chats or forums (linked from their sites).
- **CTFtime team listings:** many teams welcome new members ([teams](https://ctftime.org/)).

Be kind, never ask for flags to live competitions, and share what you learn.
